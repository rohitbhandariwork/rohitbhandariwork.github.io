# Salary Booster — Outline (v2)

Working title: **Salary Booster: The Career Leverage Playbook for Engineers**
Format: Markdown manuscript, single source for ebook output.
Target: ~25 pages. One topic per step. Practical tools, scripts, and named techniques.
Spine: the **Leverage Stack** — a three-layer model every chapter plugs into:
*Value → Visibility → Leverage*. Unifying idea: your pay is set by what decision-makers
*believe* you produce (perceived value), not what you produce.

Slug: `career-leverage`
Tagline: *The playbook for engineers who refuse to stay overlooked.*

Voice: dry, confident, second person ("you"). Chapters open with a concrete scenario,
no recurring named persona. Returns retail honesty — some levers pay more than others.

---

## Manuscript structure

### Front matter
- **Preface** — why this book exists, who it's for, one page, no hype.
- **Introduction** — the roadmap. Maps every part explicitly: "Part 1 claims the
  game; Part 2 raises your ceiling where you sit; Part 3 builds the exit lever;
  Part 4 runs the whole thing in twelve weeks."

### Part 1 — Know the Game
1. **You're not underpaid, you're undervalued.** Perceived value > value. How pay is
   truly decided, why "work hard and hope" fails, and why a meritocracy myth still
   deserves respect.
2. **How compensation actually works.** Base, bonus, equity, budget bands, who decides
   each number, which parts are negotiable, why a raise is capped and an offer is not.
3. **Know Your Number.** The market-rate ritual: research, comp data, estimate within
   10%, check quarterly, act on signal. (Named technique: *Know Your Number.*)

### Part 2 — Raise the Ceiling Where You Sit
4. **Promotions are written, not earned.** The packet, leveling rubrics, who reads
   them, impact statements in reviewer language. (Named: *The Promotion Packet.*)
5. **The Visibility Flywheel.** Artifacts, packaging, visibility without vanity,
   aligning with what leadership is measured on. (Named technique: *The Visibility Flywheel.*)
6. **Ask for it.** Sponsors, formal cycles, calibrating the ask. The sponsorship
   conversation vs. the promotion conversation, and when each happens.
7. **The counter-offer maze.** Pushback on title, band, or comp without burning
   goodwill; turning "no" into "what would it take."

### Part 3 — The Exit Leverage
8. **The resume is a sales page.** Outcome-first bullets, one page, screener keywords.
9. **Interview prep that respects effort.** The "only three kinds of questions"
   system, mock interviews, evidence bank. (Named technique: *The Evidence Bank* —
   a pre-built STAR archive.)
10. **Negotiation with known moves.** Anchoring, the *First-Number Defense*, competing
    offers, the exploding-offer defense. Scripts for every turn.
11. **The 12/24/36 model.** Evaluate comp vs. growth vs. risk on three timelines;
    when to switch and when to stay.

### Part 4 — The System
12. **The 12-week salary booster.** Week-by-week plan compiling every prior chapter:
    market number, visibility, promotion packet, or interview track. Choose a path,
    run it, review.
13. **Scripts & templates.** Email templates (comp conversation, promotion case study),
    evidence-bank sheet, offer-negotiation script, counter-offer script.

### Back matter
- **FAQ** — "Company doesn't do raises?", "Remote?", "Is quitting for a raise really
  reliable?", "What about equity/RSUs?", "Too early to negotiate?"
- **Cheat sheet** — the 5 numbers every engineer should know (market rate, walk-away
  number, target title, promotion timing, next-level exit timeline).

## Recurring boxes (every chapter)
- **Scenario opener** — one concrete engineering situation that the chapter solves.
- **Short version** — 3-4 line recap box, after every chapter (Reilly-style).
- **Do this** — exactly one concrete action, at the end of every chapter.

## Writing order
Front matter → Ch 1 → Part 2 (adjust for tone/consistency) → Part 3 → Part 4 → back matter.
Review gate: Chapter 1 approved as tone sample before the rest is written.
